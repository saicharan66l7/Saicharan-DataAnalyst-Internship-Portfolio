Statistical Validation

Hypothesis testing results

Four business questions were tested at a significance level of α = 0.05.

HypothesisTestP-valueDecision
Male AOV = Female AOVWelch's T-test0.4950Fail to Reject H0
Electronics AOV = Other Categories AOVWelch's T-test0.4137Fail to Reject H0
Weekend AOV = Weekday AOVWelch's T-test0.2757Fail to Reject H0
Category independent of GenderChi-squared Test0.0588Fail to Reject H0

The four tests did not provide statistically significant evidence for the business questions tested at the 0.05 level. One of my key learnings was that an observed difference in a chart does not automatically mean the difference is statistically significant.

Business Recommendations

Turning analysis into action

Focus on strong-performing categories

Use category performance to guide product, bundling and cross-selling opportunities.

Monitor high-performing cities

Regional sales patterns can help identify stronger markets and areas worth further investigation.

Use customer and age-group analysis

Power BI customer views can help the business understand behavior across key demographic groups.

Use evidence before major decisions

Statistical validation can help separate visible patterns from findings supported by sufficient evidence.
